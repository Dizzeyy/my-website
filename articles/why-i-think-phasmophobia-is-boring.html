Why I Think Phasmophobia Is Boring

November 11, 2026

Why do I think phasmophobia is boring?

I'm not sure.

It's an incredibly popular game.

Skip this next section if you already know what phasmo is:

In phasmophobia, you and your team of superfriends go ride a van to take pictures of footprints and see your own breath in the air for 20 minutes then take a guess at which of 30 invisible jackshit pathfinding assholes your game decided to spawn in the house.

Each one has some particular phenomenon it causes so you can sudoku your way into figuring it out if you have the patience to sit in a corner with a thermometer until your balls fall off.

843,000 ratings on steam and between 14,000 and 16,000 concurrent players at time of writing this.

A huge portion of popularity for any piece of media is the theme.

But beyond ghost hunting and occult knickknacks, phasmophobia isn't exactly dripping with atmosphere for me.

Is that unfair?

Maybe it's because the game has no music.

Technically it has lobby music, apparently, I've never really heard it.

The levels are silent,

pure audio mastering.

Which is cool, and it would make me think that I would really like it.

Excellent audio design perfects a game for me.

It's one of the reasons I so deeply love Subnautica

if you see what I did there

But in phasmophobia, crickets and hollow hallways don't exactly give me the creeps.

That's another thing about the game!

Let's compare it to an excellent game I'm probably going to write an article about sometime soon,

the excellent DOORS on Roblox, a horror game developed by LSPLASH and RediblesQW. [1]

So in DOORS, you start in a hotel. You have one goal: get to the next door.

You start walking through a series of short hallways with doors at the end.

Sometimes you have to open a cabinet to find a key,

other times the door just opens.

Eventually weird stuff starts happening, like everything shakes or some weird face screams at you.

Turns out, there are weird entities here and each one behaves in a particular way.

But you don't know how they behave, you have to learn.

So you're on door number 16 then something goes 'psst!' and you don't know what to do,

then something bites you and deals 40 damage.

And you're like what the fuck?

Oh, turns out that's screech, when he says 'psst' at you, you have to quickly turn around and look at him.

Or, the lights start flickering and you're like what the hell?

Then suddenly the room fills with black and it sounds like a subway train and you die.

Oh, turns out that's rush, when lights start to flicker you better get your ass in a cabinet or die.

It goes like that. Room after room, you learn these entities and basically navigate your way to the end.

So why do like DOORS?

Doors has juice.

In short, juice is reactivity.

It's a splash when you squash a fruit, it's a creak and a swoop of air when you open a door, it's a chime when you pick up a pickup.

Go to Brad Woods' website for a really good definition of juice and for the time of your life.

https://garden.bradwoods.io/notes/design/juice

DOORS has juice.

I think that's the best way I can describe the difference.

And also, the textures just look better.

Phasmo feels like it was built in the source engine to look realistic.

Which is fine,

whatever,

but DOORS has a nice warm tone and pieces that feel blocky, thick, and fun.

So it looks more alive.

Does Phasmo do horror better?

Does Winco do horror better than Domino's?

What kind of question is that?

Horror is preference.

And DOORS isn't horrifying.

At least, not to me.

It's thrilling.

But Phasmophobia is neither horrifying nor thrilling.

It's boring.

I've been in so many lobbies where my friends want to stop playing because they've played enough and now they're spooked out.

Phasmo isn't really juicy, either, in fact its reactivity is one of my main beefs with the game.

It seems unresponsive and glitchy.

The ghosts are never reliable, which could be argued is the point, but it's harder to argue when the ghost isn't just being tricky, it's actually not responding to an input that it should be responding to.

That kind of unreliable output randomness is frustrating, and it's the kind of frustrating that makes a game difficult to master in a way that's not the player's fault. It's just unfair and broken sometimes, which is too bad, because

it makes mastering a formal abstract system into a guessing game instead of a game about learning the game.

I'm not good at phasmophobia, I'll admit it.

But the problem isn't that I haven't mastered it.

The problem is that I don't want to master a game with inconsistent AI and dissappointingly dull visual design.

On the other hand, DOORS has pace.

DOORS is reliable. If you hear 'psst', screech is going to be there in a specific amount of time.

It's hard to master a game when your actions produce inconsistent results.

Feedback is one of the foundational requirements of 'flow', if you're into Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi's concept. I certainly am.

Check it out:

https://positivepsychology.com/mihaly-csikszentmihalyi-father-of-flow/

(There's also a famous book about it):

https://www.amazon.com/Flow-Psychology-Experience-Perennial-Classics/dp/0061339202

Flow is an excellent way to diagram out and understand positive experience, and it proves that you need a game that gives you a consistent result for each action.

It's something to think about.

Notes

[1] DOORS was inspired by Spooky's Jump Scare Mansion, Rooms by nicorocks5555, and Spelunky.
