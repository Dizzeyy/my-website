Games have an emotional octane level they try to get across in their tone, storytelling, music, atmosphere, dialogue, and game mechanics. Slow, methodical puzzles and thoughtful turn-based combat invite you to come along and try to slow down. Fast, stylistic firearm gauntlets and snappy fighting arenas invite you to breathe fast and respond with alacrity.

But you have to know that.

CONTEXT

Games deserve context when being talked about.

They deserve to be introduced alongside their intended audience.

Remember, games are like food. There is a mind-boggling variety, and lots of different tastes.

But if someone eats fourteen ghost pepper sausages then complains about how their toilet now has a scoville rating, the famous reply will always be:

"Well, what did you expect?"

That's the thing with games.

You came at a Jonathon Blow puzzle game.

What you got was a slow, lovely, cerebral puzzle game with some existential themes tied into the commentary.

And now you're complaining that the game didn't have a concrete point...

Well, what did you expect?
