I Don't Really Like Minecraft

November 10, 2026

You know,
honestly,
after playing for probably over 100 hours on multiple multiplayer servers,
trying speedrunning,
playing with myself,
on both Xbox and PC
on both Bedrock and Java,
achievement hunting,
beating the game multiple times,
trying out ragecraft IV,
and skyblock
I have to say... I'm not the biggest Minecraft fan.

I don't know why.
Maybe I'm not autistic enough.
I'm not in the 'I love trains' level.
I'm just in the 'difficult to socialize and picky eater' level.
Though I don't think it's being picky,
it's having standards.

Anyway,
C418's music shifts reality and brings you deep messages that cut to the core of the world,
which is cool.
But it's not always time to listen to Daniel, our favorite music man.
And Lena Raine's stuff is beautiful,
But after a summer of listening to Rubedo on repeat while trying to write fiction
I am done with it.

Now there are things about minecraft I like.
Wait, are there?
Hold on, let me think.
I think endermen and the end are cool.
The overworld / the nether are classic tale-as-old-as-time anthropological / folklore / mythological standards.
We've heard all that dumb bullshit before.

But the end?
That's kinda neat.
Kinda nifty.
Purple, void,
teleporting,
Kinda sweet.

Yeah, there is a dragon...
That's kinda old hat.
But I still think the ender dragon is cool, fight me.

Elytra is awesome.
And also weird.
It's a flight glider powered by fireworks where you can do dips.
And you can learn how to land well.
Did you notice how drastically your velocity stops when you make a fast break in the air?
I used to give flight lessons on one of my servers.
Back when I played minecraft, I used to do 2 things:
1. Build nether travel infrastructure
2. Fly around for fucking bozonkidonkers amounts of time in the end to collect chests of elytra for everyone on the server.
I also did other shit, actually, but those two are the cool things so I'm gonna pretend like those were my specializations.

Ender hubs kind of were my specialization.
I made ice roads like a three-legged jetpack man with liquid nitrogen diarrhea and a laxative IV
I also got interested in ice racetracks and boat racing as a game in minecraft. I think it was dope, but the problem is
that in order to get a really exciting track on a survival server you need enough ice to fill the archangel metatron's refrigerator.
And the cool stuff like lifts and weird stuff to do on tracks I never really bothered with.
It took so long just to create an N64-mario-kart-ass flat track that I stopped doing it altogether.
With special tools and a creative server it could be fun, but it does take away something when you're not gathering the resources yourself.

I also made a spleef arena.
That was fun.
Spleef is cool, it's the natural game that was created within minecraft. Like, to my knowledge no other game has a spleef.
And I'm too lazy to look up whether there is a predecessor. It would actually disappoint me to find out there is one.
I had some fun with spleef.

Basically minecraft is boring enough that I wanted to make multiplayer games on the server so we had a good time.

I've never played bedwars, and I wonder if I would enjoy it, or if it would just be lame.

It's interesting once you start to apply multiplayer game theory to games within minecraft, because suddenly all the rules that apply
to call of duty and halo apply to minecraft.

Respawn rates and respawn areas and map size and weapon damage and cover and scoring are all things that you have to take into account
if you're making a CTF map or playing some kind of team deathmatch.

And that's real fun to design and talk about, because I don't know why.

But it is, and thinking about minecraft multiplayer games rustles my jimmies.

But then I play it and I'm like okay, I'm done.

Maybe I should just design minecraft multiplayer games for other people to enjoy.

Or maybe the game modes I'm playing haven't been fun enough.

Maybe if there was a minecraft gun game I'd be interested.

The design way to make the most interesting game types is to create tension around a single objective
and to make it really hard to predict the outcome.

Let me repeat that, because it's a game design principle that I've found that proves itself again and again:
the design way to make the most interesting game types is to create TENSION around a single objective
and to make it REALLY hard to predict the outcome.

Really hard.

And vertical.

Majestic.

Dripping, with

Pizazz.

so you can sleep the fucking goddamned night and not have to deal with shit and bury yourself underground for the night to mine but you
don't know when it's daytime yet

Anyway sorry so I had this insight during a game of gears of war 2 that I was playing with a friend, and that is a story for another article
so I'll leave it there.

But yeah, I don't really like minecraft, yes I've given it a go, I like the idea of designing games within minecraft, minecraft games
follow game design principles like any other game in the genre of game you're making,
